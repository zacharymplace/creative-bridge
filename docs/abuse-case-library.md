# Abuse Case Library

Version: 1.3.0

The Abuse Case Library names foreseeable ways The Creative Bridge could be misused, weakened, or distorted.

The standard should name abuse before it happens.

## Abuse cases

### Dream-team pressure loop

Risk: A creator publicly names ideal collaborators to generate attention, creating pressure on those people to respond.

Guardrail: Dream-team references must stay private, opt-in, and non-public unless a collaborator explicitly agrees.

### Fake human legitimacy

Risk: A project uses a minor human contribution to imply the work is substantially human-made.

Guardrail: Human contributions should be credited specifically, with clear separation from AI-assisted scaffolding.

### Hidden AI production pipeline

Risk: AI materially shapes visuals, storyboards, pitch materials, or production assets while public copy implies manual authorship.

Guardrail: Material AI use should be disclosed where it affects creative development or production claims.

### Style impersonation laundering

Risk: A project avoids naming a living artist directly but still markets itself around a recognizable copied style.

Guardrail: The rejected patterns registry should treat indirect style impersonation as a live risk, not a loophole.

### Marketplace drift

Risk: Preparation templates slowly become public listings, ranking, hiring flows, or pay-to-access collaboration surfaces.

Guardrail: Any marketplace, payment, ranking, messaging, or public profile behavior requires explicit governance review.

### Readiness as certification

Risk: A self-assessment score gets marketed as approval, verification, endorsement, or safety certification.

Guardrail: Readiness language must remain non-certifying and should clearly state what has not been verified.

### Pressure-based recruitment

Risk: Creators use urgency, social pressure, fandom, public callouts, or emotional framing to recruit collaborators.

Guardrail: Outreach should be consent-aware, non-public by default, and free of pressure mechanics.

### Collaborator bait-and-switch

Risk: A project asks for advisory help, then expands the expected labor without pay, scope clarity, or credit agreement.

Guardrail: Role requests must define collaboration type, scope, deliverables, credit, compensation, and boundaries.

### Review theater

Risk: Public review exists in name only while decisions continue to prioritize speed, growth, or optics.

Guardrail: Governance decisions should remain documented, versioned, and tied to specific risks or rejected patterns.

### AI asset dumping

Risk: The project becomes a volume feed of AI outputs instead of a bridge toward human collaboration.

Guardrail: Project profiles should require intent, role clarity, disclosure, boundaries, and a human-finished pathway.
