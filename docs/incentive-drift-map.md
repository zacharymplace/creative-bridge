# Incentive Drift Map

Version: 1.4.0

The Incentive Drift Map names pressures that could gradually pull The Creative Bridge away from its stated purpose.

The project is most at risk when harmful behavior becomes convenient, profitable, measurable, or socially rewarded.

## Drift pressures

### Engagement pressure

Risk: The project starts rewarding provocative AI outputs, controversy, public callouts, or creator drama because they drive attention.

Counterpressure: Prioritize review quality, disclosure clarity, consent boundaries, and rejected-pattern avoidance over engagement metrics.

### Marketplace pressure

Risk: Preparation tools slowly become public listings, paid placement, ranking, matching, or hiring flows.

Counterpressure: Keep marketplace behavior behind explicit governance review and separate preparation from transactions.

### Creator monetization pressure

Risk: Creators are pushed to package rough AI-assisted prototypes as sellable products before human craft, rights, credit, and consent are resolved.

Counterpressure: Require clear tiering, AI-use disclosure, role needs, and human-finished pathways before commercial framing.

### Collaborator scarcity pressure

Risk: Limited access to artists, editors, producers, or advisors becomes a reason to pressure people publicly or normalize speculative unpaid labor.

Counterpressure: Keep collaborator interest opt-in, private by default, scope-specific, and clear about compensation or non-compensation.

### Ranking pressure

Risk: Rubrics or readiness checks become scores, leaderboards, badges, or pseudo-certification.

Counterpressure: Keep review language qualitative, non-certifying, non-ranking, and explicit about what has not been verified.

### Platform lock-in pressure

Risk: The standard becomes dependent on proprietary accounts, hosted assets, private workflows, or opaque moderation decisions.

Counterpressure: Keep canonical standards, docs, schemas, and release notes portable, static, and publicly inspectable.

### Growth-team pressure

Risk: The project reframes boundaries as friction and starts optimizing for signups, retention, or creator throughput.

Counterpressure: Treat boundaries as core product value, not onboarding friction.

### Aesthetic novelty pressure

Risk: The project rewards visually striking AI output even when disclosure, consent, attribution, or human-finished pathways are weak.

Counterpressure: Do not let polish outrank process integrity.

### Institutional authority pressure

Risk: The standard starts presenting itself as an authority that certifies safe, ethical, or legitimate AI-assisted work.

Counterpressure: Keep the project framed as a public standard and review system, not a certification body.

### Convenience pressure

Risk: Automated defaults slowly replace explicit human judgment because they make review, matching, scoring, or publishing faster.

Counterpressure: Require governance review before adding automation that changes visibility, access, ranking, moderation, or claims.
